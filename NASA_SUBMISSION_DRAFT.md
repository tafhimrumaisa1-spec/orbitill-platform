## Project

**ORBITILL: RootShift**

**Working tagline:** One field. Three futures. A changing planet.

## Short summary

ORBITILL turns NASA Earth observations into transparent questions about what a farm could grow next. Its first practical research demonstration compares a current Rajshahi/Barind crop pathway with two alternatives, recomputes water implications when rainfall or irrigation assumptions change, and exposes every source, assumption and limitation behind the result.

## The problem

Farm decisions must reconcile weather, water access, soils, crop timing, costs and local priorities. Satellite observations can reveal environmental context, but they cannot by themselves prescribe what a farmer should plant. Bangladesh also contains sharply different agricultural realities: the drought-prone Barind Highlands and salinity-affected coastal systems require different evidence and different constraints.

## The solution

ORBITILL combines a global capability explorer with evidence-gated regional research pilots. In the Barind demonstration, verified offline NASA POWER daily observations enter a crop-water calculation. A user compares T. Aman→Boro, T. Aman→Wheat and T. Aman→Mustard pathways under shared assumptions, changes rainfall or irrigation availability, and receives recalculated water implications with a new model-run identity. Unsupported soil, calendar, economic and coastal claims remain conditional or unavailable.

## NASA data

NASA POWER daily precipitation, temperature, humidity, wind and solar-radiation variables provide the historical environmental series used by the Barind calculation. The interface discloses the observation window, retrieval time, parameter names, spatial support and limitations. NASA POWER represents a regional grid cell, not a field sensor.

## Technology

- EarthSync normalizes NASA observations and preserves provenance.
- AquaSense performs deterministic crop-water simulations.
- SoilBrain gates crop and soil rules according to regional evidence.
- RootShift compares declared pathways without collapsing trade-offs into an opaque score.
- FutureFields defines hypothetical scenario changes.
- TerraLens provides integrated capability-aware regional exploration with offline boundaries and explicit readiness gates.
- EvidenceLayer connects every visible result to its run, inputs and sources.

## Local and global relevance

Rajshahi/Barind is the first practical demonstration, not the platform’s geographic limit. Bangladesh-wide exploration distinguishes Barind and coastal evidence needs. Iowa is a separate United States research pilot. Other global locations expose environmental context and readiness gaps without pretending that one agronomic model is valid everywhere.

## Limitations

ORBITILL has not been field-deployed or validated as farm advice. The current Barind comparison demonstrates water implications under explicit soil, efficiency and calendar assumptions; it does not predict yield or profit. Coastal recommendations remain disabled without dated salinity evidence.

## What is next

Work with local agronomists and farmers to validate crop calendars, soil parameters, irrigation constraints and economics; add dated coastal salinity evidence; and evaluate whether the interface improves the clarity—not merely the quantity—of information used in seasonal planning.
